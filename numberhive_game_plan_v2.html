Number Hive · 9-Month Bridge

Game Plan (v2 · loose)

For James + the team. Working draft — updated to reflect the DfE EdTech market report (June 2026) and Fletch's market read. Full model, wireframes, flows & matrix live in the applet (numberhive_architecture_fixed.html).

01 The bet, in one line

The product is loved; the funnel is broken. Build a free, play-first front door that lets students and teachers experience the magic in two minutes, then prove it can create repeat play, sharing and school-qualified demand before we build the rest.

The free game is not only a school funnel. If the player loop catches fire, it may become its own consumer channel. The job of the MVP is to preserve that upside while making sure school value can still be captured.
Player layer
Fun, fast, viral, competitive.
Teacher layer
Class challenge, basic insight, launch pathway.
School layer
Evidence, reporting, implementation, paid value.

02 Why this, why now

  • ~£2–3k MRR, subs-only. Once teachers use it, they love it. The bottleneck is getting them to see the value.
  • The three frictions this kills:
    • Sales calls blocked by admin gatekeepers → product-led, bottom-up: a teacher adopts by playing; the school deal follows.
    • Teachers won't evaluate a tool → they play a 2-minute game, not sit a demo.
    • No automated school process (promo-code hack) → School super-admin replaces it (fast-follow).
    • Confusing onboarding → "just play," no setup.
  • Assets we're not using enough: ~15k educator emails; a genuinely loved & differentiated product; and a strong "goes beyond recall" story — schools and kids already love competitive maths-fact practice (TTRS proves the appetite), which de-risks Hive Clash. (Keep the TTRS comparison internal/soft — see §10.)

Market timing DfE report

The June 2026 DfE/PUBLIC market assessment points the same direction we're already moving:

  • The English EdTech market is maturing — schools are more evidence-driven and more selective.
  • Buyers are shifting from "interesting technology" toward tools that demonstrate impact, reduce workload, align with curriculum priorities and support implementation.
  • Procurement is increasingly influenced by senior leaders, MATs, curriculum leads, digital-strategy leads and trust-level decision-makers — and centralised at trust level.
  • The report describes our exact pain (educator-built SMEs drowned out by big marketing budgets; schools overwhelmed by free trials) and shows word-of-mouth / peer networks are what actually drive adoption when formal evidence is thin — i.e. our share loop is the documented mechanism.
The market is moving toward Number Hive, not away from it. Schools increasingly want tools that demonstrate educational impact, reduce teacher workload, fit curriculum priorities and support consistent implementation across a school or trust. This validates the shift from "fun mathematics games" to a whole-school mathematics fluency and improvement platform.
AI is not the main threat here. Generic AI can help teachers plan lessons, but it does not replace high-frequency fluency practice, deliberate retrieval, strategic application of number facts, healthy competition, long-term mastery tracking or whole-school participation. (Public wording: prefer "intelligent practice pathways / targeted practice / adaptive recommendations / teacher insight / fluency data" — don't call it "AI practice" yet.)

03 Strategic positioning after the DfE report

From "Fun mathematics games" → To "a game-based mathematics fluency platform that helps schools build confident mathematicians through engaging practice, measurable progress and whole-school participation."

Build fluency. Grow confidence. See progress.

The positioning hierarchy

  • Students love it.
  • Therefore they practise more.
  • Therefore fluency, confidence and strategic thinking grow.
  • Teachers can see progress.
  • Schools can build a culture of mathematical improvement.
Do not reduce Number Hive to dashboards. The game is not just a delivery mechanism; the game is the pedagogy. But the data is what helps school leaders justify the purchase.

04 The model

  • Two roles: Player & Admin. One gate: free to play, paid to manage, assess and act (one boolean per hive). One world, umbrella-contained (minors under a teacher/parent umbrella). Tiers = price brackets (Single / Group / Class / School), not feature gates.
The B2C player experience must be allowed to stand on its own. Players should not feel like they're being pushed into a school product. The school layer should sit underneath and beside the player loop — not in front of it.
  • Gameplay and data generation are free.
  • Player identity, wins, challenges, Hive IQ-style progress and sharing sit in the player world.
  • Teacher / class functionality sits in the bridge layer.
  • School insight, reporting, intervention signal, assessment, implementation and management sit in the paid admin layer.
  • Public player data must be safe, minimal and child-appropriate.
  • Student data visible to teachers must be education-focused and privacy-conscious.
  • Hive IQ / personal competitive rating stays personal and player-facing — never part of a teacher-judgement report.

05 The 6-week MVP — prove the mouth and the bridge

Goal: test whether the free front door can create repeat play and sharing, while also testing whether that attention can generate teacher / class / school intent. This is not the full monetisation build — it's the smallest test of the growth loop and the school bridge.

Ship

  • Free instant-play game (no account) — the magic, in 2 minutes.
  • The viral loops: share-your-win card (players, public), invite-a-friend (host / join / play & pass), the profile hub.
  • Light account + the player home.
  • Lean conversion: let early teacher converts use the existing product/gate — don't rebuild it yet.
  • Heavy instrumentation: activation, D1/D7 retention, K (invites + conversion), per-loop — and the school-bridge signals (§06).

Add the tiny teacher bridge new

  • Teacher-facing landing path connected to the free game.
  • Post-play CTA: "Run this with your class" / "Create a class challenge."
  • Simple class challenge link / code.
  • Basic teacher view: how many students joined / played.
  • CTA to request a School Hive, pilot pack or upgrade info.
  • Automated follow-up email to teachers after class activity.
  • Keep it lightweight — no full dashboard rebuild in the 6-week MVP.
Don't let the teacher bridge slow the player experience. For players, the primary CTAs stay challenge a friend, play again, share a win, climb / improve. Teacher prompts appear contextually and lightly.
Launch framing. The 15k list is mostly educators — so don't launch as "try our new free game." Launch as "Give your students a 2-minute fluency challenge," "Launch a free class challenge," "See why students keep coming back." Seed both player activity and teacher-led classroom activity. Sequence and segment the list (teachers · leaders · existing users · inactive · parents/home) — never one blast.
Still NOT in 6 weeks: ranked Hive Clash (unless it becomes a very clear unlock after initial signal) · full School super-admin automation · rebuilt teacher reporting · complex dashboards · deep integrations · cosmetics/seasons · full current-product redesign. We are patching the school pathway, not rebuilding the whole school product yet.
💬 To discuss — exactly how prominent the teacher bridge is without taxing consumer purity; and whether ranked Hive Clash is really deferrable when it may be the main retention engine. See §15.1, §15.3.

06 How we judge it

Anchor metric: activated players = played ≥1 full game and came back once. (Not raw "plays.")

Player gates — these matter most

SignalMinimum to imply it works
Activation (arrivals → full game)≥ 50–60%
D7 retention≥ 20–25%
K (viral coefficient)≥ 0.5 and trending up

School bridge signals new

SignalWhat it means
Teacher activationTeacher creates a class challenge or class link.
Class spread10+ students play through one teacher link / code.
Teacher returnTeacher comes back after student activity to see what happened.
School intentTeacher clicks report, pilot, pricing, School Hive or upgrade.
Commercial signalSchool Hive conversation, pilot request or quote created.
Don't judge the MVP on raw player numbers alone. A smaller number of players with strong teacher/class conversion may be more commercially valuable than a larger number of isolated casual players.
Separate the B2C signal from the school signal. If B2C flies but school intent is weak → preserve the consumer opportunity and redesign the school bridge. If school intent is strong but virality is modest → strengthen the teacher-led classroom challenge path.
💬 To discuss — what's the single primary signal that means "go"? And note the test is rigged: a teacher-list launch under-tests cold consumer virality — do we carve out a non-teacher seed to read it honestly? See §15.1, §15.2.

Two curves (player reach)

🟢 Stretch / "hit" — needs K ≈ 0.75–0.9

⚪ Minimum / "alive" — K ≥ 0.5, D7 ≥ 20%

Week 2≥ 500

Tripwires

🟢Green. B2C loop is spreading and the school bridge is producing teacher/class signals → lean in (ranked, more loops, maybe a little spend).
🟡Yellow. Either B2C or the teacher bridge is working, but not both yet → don't overreact; tune the weak side.
🔴Red. Low activation, low retention, low sharing and low teacher intent → fix onboarding / magic / positioning before building more.
A weak 6-week viral result does not automatically invalidate the strategy. It may mean the email list was cold, the seed audience was wrong, the teacher framing was off, the share loop was weak, or the challenge wasn't clear enough. Diagnose before you pivot.
The maths to watch: K = i × c (invites per user × invite→signup rate). Amplification on any seed = 1 / (1 − K) — K=0.6 turns 1 player into 2.5, K=0.8 into 5. We don't need K>1; we need it as high as we can push it. Keep viral cycle time short — loops fire right after a match. Judge the slope and loop quality, not the headline number.

08 How much do we fix the current product?

Enough to stop warm demand leaking. Not enough to distract from testing the new growth hypothesis.

Rule: fix current-product friction only where it directly protects revenue or converts new demand.

Worth doing now

  • Clearer teacher onboarding
  • Simple class setup / student join
  • Clearer "what to do first"
  • Basic reporting clarity
  • School Hive positioning
  • Pricing / upgrade path clarity
  • Privacy / procurement page
  • Admin / billing fixes that cut manual chaos

Not worth doing now

  • Full redesign
  • Perfect dashboards
  • Complex integrations
  • Full school-admin automation before signal
  • Advanced reporting rebuild
  • Every onboarding edge case
  • Cosmetic polish that doesn't lift activation/conversion

09 The 9-month bridge — loose sequence

Weeks 0–6 · MVP
Ship the free game + loops + instrumentation + tiny teacher bridge. Launch to the 15k with teacher-led challenge framing, not generic consumer-game framing.
Weeks 6–12 · Read, tune & bridge
Measure B2C health and school-bridge signals separately. Tune share/invite loops. Improve the teacher challenge flow. Start/continue the impact report from existing data. Fast-follow ranked Hive Clash only if the player loop justifies it.
Weeks 12–24 · Build conversion (if 🟢/🟡)
Polish the gate. Build/automate School super-admin if there's enough teacher/school signal. Harden reporting around the insights schools actually value. Improve onboarding from observed friction. Turn implementation into a product asset.
Weeks 24–36 · Scale what works
Lean into whichever shows the strongest evidence: B2C consumer growth, teacher-led classroom challenges, whole-school fluency campaigns, MAT/district expansion — or a combination. Consider a small efficacy study or formal pilot if the data justifies it.
Throughout
Preserve optionality. Don't force the B2C game to become merely a school funnel; don't let consumer excitement distract from school revenue. Keep both lanes visible. Fletch (5h) on owned content/positioning + impact report; Jono (20h) on ops/billing/school process.

11 The social campaign — the warm-up new

The launch isn't a single "it's live" post — it's a 3-week, organic, story-led warm-up that earns belief before it asks for anything. Arc: Believe it → See it → Play it. Week 1 sells the idea (recall ≠ fluency); week 2 shows the game and the proof; week 3 builds the day-one crowd.

  • Why this shape: the DfE read says word-of-mouth and peer networks drive adoption when formal evidence is thin — so we lead with a belief worth sharing, not a hard sell. The free game is the word-of-mouth artifact.
  • One belief, three audiences: teachers (the engine + amplifiers — "stop drilling, start applying"), parents ("screen time you don't feel bad about"), and maths-curious adults ("chess, but built on numbers"). Same truth, three outfits.
  • Channels (organic only): Instagram/Facebook · X/LinkedIn · email + community. Realistic founder cadence; repurpose one idea across surfaces rather than inventing five.
  • Proof we already own: real testimonials from Pam Harris, Dan Finkel and Liesl McConchie — recognised maths-ed names, front-and-centre in the Proof pillar.
  • Launch framing (ties to §05): to the 15k it's "give your students a 2-minute fluency challenge," not "try our new game" — seeding both player and classroom activity. Sequence and segment; never one blast.
Built & ready to run: the campaign plan (numberhive_social_campaign.html), every post drafted to final (numberhive_posts_final.md), and an 8-card branded graphics pack incl. the refreshed free-to-play quote (numberhive_social_graphics.html). Gameplay-clip selection pending raw video.
💬 To discuss — who actually runs the campaign (Fletch's 5h enough?); which market we lead in (UK/AU "maths" vs the US "math" of our existing testimonials); and whether we hold a small paid budget in reserve to amplify whatever organic content over-performs. See §15.4, §15.5.

12 Privacy & trust

Privacy and trust are now purchasing criteria — make our relatively lightweight data model visible, not assumed.
  • No public chat.
  • Safe generated handles for minors.
  • Minimal public student data.
  • Teacher-only student insight.
  • Clear data-protection / privacy page.
  • Child-safe design is part of the product, not an afterthought.

15 Things to discuss — as a team open

The genuinely open calls — where the plan has a view but not a decision. Leans below are starting points to argue with, not conclusions.

A · Focus & how we read the 6-week test
  • 15.1 — What is the single primary signal that means "go"? The plan preserves a lot of optionality (if B2C flies do X, if school flies do Y). For a 2-person build that's a luxury — the real enemy is a muddy result we can't read. Pick the one number that decides it. Lean: player activation × K as the primary "is the magic real and spreadable" gate; school intent as the secondary "can we capture it" gate.
  • 15.2 — Do we carve out a non-teacher seed to test cold virality? Launching to 15k teachers tests the bridge well but under-tests true consumer spread. If B2C looks modest at wk6 we won't know if it failed or was never tested. Lean: yes — a small organic/Reddit/Discord seed purely to read stranger-to-stranger sharing, kept separate in the data.
  • 15.6 — What counts as "school-qualified demand"? Define the commercial bar up front (e.g. N pilot/quote requests, or X teachers reaching "class spread") so we're not arguing about it after the fact.
B · Product scope & sequencing
  • 15.3 — Is ranked Hive Clash really deferrable, and how prominent is the teacher bridge? Ranked is the heaviest build but may be the actual retention engine — defer it and D7 might read low simply because the sticky loop isn't shipped. And every teacher CTA is a small tax on consumer purity. Lean: keep ranked out, lean on async challenge for retention, and don't red-light on D7 alone; keep teacher prompts contextual/light.
  • 15.7 — Which current-product fixes actually make the 20%? "Fix only what blocks revenue/conversion" is the rule — but we need the specific shortlist so warm demand doesn't leak through confusing onboarding while we chase the new loop.
  • 15.8 — Lock the definition of "activated." Played a full game and returned once? Agree it now so the headline metric isn't reinterpreted later.
C · Positioning, market & go-to-market
  • 15.9 — What can we claim publicly now vs later? "Whole-school fluency platform" is fundable and right, but the evidence is anecdotal and the market's bar is rising. Agree a public claim ceiling ("indicators, not proof") that everyone — including on live sales calls — holds to.
  • 15.10 — Do we name TTRS publicly at all? "Goes beyond recall" is agreed; the open question is whether we ever reference TTRS by name (schools may like it) or stay purely positive. Lean: don't name them publicly; use the comparison only internally and in nuanced sales conversations.
  • 15.4 — Who runs the social campaign? It's built and ready, but organic still needs a consistent hand. Is Fletch's 5h enough, does Chris front the founder voice, or do we need more?
  • 15.5 — Which market do we lead in, and do we hold paid budget in reserve? UK/AU "maths" vs the US "math" of our existing testimonials and the UK-specific DfE report — pick a lead market for launch framing. And decide now whether a small paid budget waits in the wings to amplify over-performing organic.
  • 15.11 — What usable evidence / case studies do we have right now? The evidence pack assumes pre/post Buzz data, testimonials and the Allen County story — confirm what's actually available so the impact report isn't blocked on missing inputs.
The strategy is not B2C instead of schools. It's B2C as the demand engine, school insight as the revenue engine, and evidence/implementation as the trust engine.
The game wins attention. The bridge captures intent. The evidence builds trust. The school product captures revenue.
