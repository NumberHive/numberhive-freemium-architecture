Why Network Effects Change Everything
Within Classes
Each student who joins makes the hive more competitive — increasing engagement for everyone already there.
Between Teachers
A teacher seeing another class's results is pulled in by peer curiosity — organic B2B referral with no sales team.
Across Schools
Inter-school competition creates institutional adoption. The product spreads district-wide without a rep.
Player to Player
Global ELO means students want to climb the ladder at home. Hive Gym + Hive Clash turns every player into a recruiter.
Hive Clash
A dual-engine growth mechanism — institutionally controlled for schools, virally open for everyone else.

⚡ Global ELO Ranking

Like chess.com or Clash Royale — one global ladder, every player ranked.
🌍 Global, not local. Students compete against every Number Hive player, not just their classmates. The leaderboard has meaning because it has scale.
📈 Self-motivating. ELO creates intrinsic drive to improve and climb — students play outside school hours without being asked.
🎯 Shareable status. "I'm ranked 847" is something students tell friends. Rankings become social currency — the viral hook.
🎓 Teacher controlled. Teachers can enable or disable Hive Clash per hive — full institutional control with no friction for adoption.
🔓 Open to B2C. Free users (no school, no teacher) can play Hive Clash too — global ELO ties B2B and B2C together.
Dual Growth Engine
B2B Engine
Classroom → School
Teacher enables Hive Clash. Students compete within and across classes. Inter-class and inter-school rivalry drives organic teacher referral and school-wide adoption.
B2C Engine
Student → Friend
Global ELO means students want to play at home. Challenge links pull in friends who have no school connection — arriving as free B2C users and feeding back into B2B when their teachers discover the product.
