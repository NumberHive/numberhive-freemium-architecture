Product Features & Subscription Model

Two worlds — Public Game for open play, Educator Game for classrooms — connected by a teacher-transfer bridge.
Two Worlds, One Product
Number Hive operates as two connected experiences. Players enter through the Public Game. Teachers enter the Educator Game — free to start, with subscription tiers that unlock classroom power and school-wide administration.

Entry
Anyone can play immediately with zero friction. The public game is the top of the funnel — no registration, no payment, no school needed.
Bridge
Public players who are teachers can transfer into the Educator Game. They already know the product — conversion to educator is high-intent.
Escalation
Educator tiers unlock progressively. Free → Classroom ($) → School ($$$). Each level adds administrative power and reporting depth.
Students
Student gameplay is identical across all tiers. The subscription determines what the educator can see and manage — never what the student can play.
Core Model
The public game creates players. The educator game creates subscribers. The bridge connects them. Gameplay is always free — insight and administration are paid.
Public Game
Open play for anyone. The public game has two tiers — anonymous instant play and registered play with expanded features.
👤

Anonymous Player

No registration · instant play
Play vs Basic AI Single-player against a standard-difficulty bot
Instant access No account, no email, no sign-up — just play
Session-only progress Nothing persists between visits

Registered Player

Free registration · persistent account
Everything anonymous, plus…
vs Local Player Play against someone on the same device
+ vs Remote Player Online multiplayer against other registered players
+ Persistent Progress Record Stats, history, and progress saved across sessions
+ vs Advanced AI Harder AI opponents for experienced players

Why Two Tiers?

🚪
Zero-friction entry. Anonymous play removes every barrier. The game sells itself before we ask for an email.
🎯
Registration is earned. Players register because they want multiplayer and progress — not because we force them.
🌉
Teacher bridge. Registered players who are teachers see the "Bring this to your class" CTA — the transfer point to the Educator Game.

Public Game Scope

🎮
Gameplay only. No hives, no student management, no reporting. Pure competitive play.
📊
No analytics. Public players see their own stats (if registered) but no classroom-style reporting exists here.
🔄
Acquisition engine. Every public player is a potential teacher lead, student recruit, or word-of-mouth amplifier.
Educator Game
Three tiers for educators — free to start, classroom subscription for power users, school subscription for institutional deployment.
🎓

Educator — Free

Free registration · no credit card
1× Hive Create and manage a single class hive
Anonymous Student Accounts Students join with name + hive code — no email required
vs Other Students Students compete within the hive
vs Basic AI Students can practice against standard bots
Educator subscribes $ →
📘

Classroom Subscription

$ · per educator per term / year
Everything in Free, plus…
Multiple Hives Create hives for different classes, year groups, or ability sets
Intra-hive Competency Reporting See fluency profiles, growth trends, and skill gaps within each hive
+ Classroom Functionality Assign skills, run competitions, export reports, heat maps
Educator subscribes $$$ →
🏫

School Subscription

$$$ · school-wide licence
Everything in Classroom, plus…
Sub-educators Add and manage multiple teachers under one school account
Inter-hive Competency Reporting Compare performance across hives, classes, and year groups
Multiple Hives (school-wide) All educators under the school licence manage their own hives
+ School Functionality School-level admin, cross-class analytics, cohort tracking

Upgrade Triggers

📈
Free → Classroom: Teacher hits the 1-hive limit, or wants to see competency data that's been accumulating silently.
🏫
Classroom → School: Multiple teachers at the same school are subscribing individually. School licence consolidates and saves.

Design Principles

🎮
Student play is identical regardless of which tier the educator is on. Subscriptions gate the educator's view, not the student's experience.
🔒
Data accumulates at every tier. Free educators generate competency data — they just can't read it until they subscribe.

Key Insight
The Public Game and Educator Game are separate feature sets with no overlap in management tools. A public player never sees hive management; an educator's students never see public multiplayer. They're connected only by the teacher-transfer bridge.

Public → Educator Transfer

🔄
High-intent conversion. Teachers who transfer from the public game already understand the product — they've played it. Time-to-value in the educator game is dramatically shorter.
📱
CTA surfaces post-session. After playing, a result screen asks "Are you a teacher? Bring this to your class →" — the bridge is always visible but never blocking.

Subscription Escalation

📊
Data is the trigger. Free educators see that data is being collected — they feel it accumulating. The subscription unlocks the view, not the collection.
🏫
School is consolidation. When 3+ teachers at one school are on Classroom plans, the School licence becomes the obvious next step — cheaper per-seat, with cross-class visibility.
