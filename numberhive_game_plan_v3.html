01 The bet, in one line

The product is loved; the funnel is broken. Build a free, play-first front door that lets students and teachers experience the magic in two minutes, then prove it can create repeat play, sharing and school-qualified demand before we build the rest.

The free game is not only a school funnel. If the player loop catches fire, it may become its own consumer channel. The job of the MVP is to preserve that upside while making sure school value can still be captured.
Player layer
Fun, fast, viral, competitive.
Teacher layer
Class challenge, basic insight, launch pathway.
School layer
Evidence, reporting, implementation, paid value.

02 Why this, why now

  • ~£2–3k MRR, subs-only. Once teachers use it, they love it. The bottleneck is getting them to see the value.
  • The three frictions this kills:
    • Sales calls blocked by admin gatekeepers → product-led, bottom-up: a teacher adopts by playing; the school deal follows.
    • Teachers won't evaluate a tool → they play a 2-minute game, not sit a demo.
    • No automated school process (promo-code hack) → School super-admin replaces it (fast-follow).
    • Confusing onboarding → "just play," no setup.
  • Assets we're not using enough: ~15k educator emails; a genuinely loved & differentiated product; and a strong "goes beyond recall" story — schools and kids already love competitive maths-fact practice (TTRS proves the appetite), which de-risks Hive Clash. (Keep the TTRS comparison internal/soft — see §14.)

Market timing DfE report

The June 2026 DfE/PUBLIC market assessment points the same direction we're already moving:

  • The English EdTech market is maturing — schools are more evidence-driven and more selective.
  • Buyers are shifting from "interesting technology" toward tools that demonstrate impact, reduce workload, align with curriculum priorities and support implementation.
  • Procurement is increasingly influenced by senior leaders, MATs, curriculum leads, digital-strategy leads and trust-level decision-makers — and centralised at trust level.
  • The report describes our exact pain (educator-built SMEs drowned out by big marketing budgets; schools overwhelmed by free trials) and shows word-of-mouth / peer networks are what actually drive adoption when formal evidence is thin — i.e. our share loop is the documented mechanism.
The market is moving toward Number Hive, not away from it. Schools increasingly want tools that demonstrate educational impact, reduce teacher workload, fit curriculum priorities and support consistent implementation across a school or trust. This validates the shift from "fun mathematics games" to a whole-school mathematics fluency and improvement platform.
AI is not the main threat here. Generic AI can help teachers plan lessons, but it does not replace high-frequency fluency practice, deliberate retrieval, strategic application of number facts, healthy competition, long-term mastery tracking or whole-school participation. (Public wording: prefer "intelligent practice pathways / targeted practice / adaptive recommendations / teacher insight / fluency data" — don't call it "AI practice" yet.)

04 Two stacks, one frictionless CTA updated 21 Jul

We should think of this as two stacks connected by a direct prompt — a Public game and the existing edu product — not three products with a bridge feature in between. People and conviction cross the gap; data never does (a public player's history never becomes classroom data, and vice versa). The B2C front door is where players experience the magic. The existing B2B product is where schools get deeper assessment, reporting, implementation and paid value. There is no separate intermediate layer anymore — a teacher moves from personal play straight into the edu product via an in-game popup or prompt at the moment they're most bought in, with no extra setup step in between.

1 · B2C Game Layer
Purpose: fun, fast, viral, low-friction play.
  • Play vs bot · play vs friend · play-and-pass (if useful)
  • Invite / challenge loop · share / win card
  • Light account · player home / profile · retention loops
  • Ranked Hive Clash later, if the signal justifies it
2 · B2B School Layer
Purpose: paid school value.
  • Existing teacher / class product · Buzz / assessment · reporting
  • Learning Journeys · intervention insight · School Hive
  • Paid admin / management layer · implementation assets
  • Privacy / procurement support · whole-school fluency campaign
The bridge is not a feature anymore. The bridge is a well-timed CTA. Exactly how that CTA works, what triggers it, what a teacher sees on the other side of it, is still being refined. This section states the direction, not the finished spec.

05 The model

  • Two roles: Player & Admin. One gate: free to play, paid to manage, assess and act (one boolean per hive). One world, umbrella-contained (minors under a teacher/parent umbrella). Tiers = price brackets (Single / Group / Class / School), not feature gates.
The B2C player experience must be allowed to stand on its own. Players should not feel like they're being pushed into a school product. The school layer should sit underneath and beside the player loop — not in front of it.
  • Gameplay and data generation are free.
  • Player identity, wins, challenges, Hive IQ-style progress and sharing sit in the player world.
  • Teacher / class functionality sits in the bridge layer.
  • School insight, reporting, intervention signal, assessment, implementation and management sit in the paid admin layer.
  • Public player data must be safe, minimal and child-appropriate.
  • Student data visible to teachers must be education-focused and privacy-conscious.
  • Hive IQ / personal competitive rating stays personal and player-facing — never part of a teacher-judgement report.

How the bridge actually works updated 21 Jul

The bridge is not a separate feature the player has to set up. The bridge is: B2C creates conviction, and a well-timed CTA turns that conviction straight into edu-product usage, with nothing in between.

1Teacher plays the B2C game→ 2notices the educational richness→ 3sees a prompt, "try this with your class"→ 4one click, straight into the edu product
The B2C MVP does not need to become the B2B product. It should remain clean: play, enjoy, challenge, return. The CTA into the edu product should be just as thin as the old bridge was meant to be, just without a separate object sitting in between. Exact placement, timing and wording of that CTA is still being worked out.
Core principle for this version: let the B2C game stay pure enough to fly, but connected enough that teacher conviction can become school demand.

06 Teacher play — the hidden bridge new

Teachers are not just administrators or buyers — teachers can be genuine players in the network. One of Number Hive's unusual strengths is adult playability: teachers play it themselves, enjoy it, and then quickly notice the educational richness. The adoption pathway is not "read the pedagogy, then try the product." It is "play first, notice the strategy, recognise the learning, then want it for students."

The Teacher Realisation Loop

1plays casually→ 2enjoys the game→ 3notices the maths→ 4"I've got to get my kids playing this"→ 5launches a Class Challenge→ 6students play→ 7teacher / school conversation begins

Step 3 in full: factors, products, blocking, strategy, fluency, reasoning, number sense — the richness surfaces through play, not through a pitch.

Teacher play becomes teacher conviction.
Teachers are not just the route to students. Teachers are part of the player network.

The Staffroom-to-Classroom Loop

Number Hive has a staffroom-to-classroom loop that most EdTech products do not have. Teachers play it with students, then play it with colleagues. That creates internal champions, social proof, and whole-school conversation before a formal sales process begins.

teacher plays→ challenges a colleague→ staffroom competition→ internal champion→ class challenge→ whole-school conversation

Marketing lines to test (copy only — does not require a staffroom mode in the MVP):

  • "Fun enough for the staffroom. Rich enough for the classroom."
  • "The game teachers play first — then bring to their students."
  • "Play it once and you'll see why your students need it."
  • "Built for students. Addictive enough for the staffroom."
  • "When teachers keep playing after class, you know it's different."

07 The 6-week MVP — prove the mouth and the bridge

Goal: test whether the free front door can create repeat play and sharing, while also testing whether that attention can generate teacher / class / school intent. This is not the full monetisation build — it's the smallest test of the growth loop and the school bridge.

Ship

  • Free instant-play game (no account) — the magic, in 2 minutes.
  • The viral loops: share-your-win card (players, public), invite / challenge (host / join / play & pass), the profile hub. The same challenge-link mechanic carries multiple contexts — challenge a friend · challenge a colleague · staffroom challenge · class challenge — as copy variations, not separate modes.
  • Profile-less by default + optional Google sign-in (no passwords) to protect your Hive IQ — an exchange, not a gate, prompted on an achievement unlock, never upfront. Player home/profile.
  • Lean conversion: let early teacher converts use the existing product/gate — don't rebuild it yet.
  • Heavy instrumentation: activation, D1/D7 retention, K (invites + conversion), per-loop — and the school-bridge signals (§10).
The two numbers. Hive IQ (HiQ) — a vs-AI Elo rating anchored to the rival ladder (Bumble → Queen Beatrix), live from day one, personal (never in an admin's report). Challenge Score — the per-seed "beat my score" share ("same board, same rival — beat it?"). BUZZ score is retired; the classroom Buzz assessment is a separate thing and stays. Ranked Hive Clash later reuses the Hive IQ scale.
Do not build a separate staffroom mode yet. Let the same challenge-link mechanic carry friend, colleague and class contexts. These are mostly copy/context variations on one mechanic in the first build.

Add the thin Class Challenge bridge new

  • Teacher-facing landing path connected to the free game.
  • Post-play CTA: "Run this with your class" / "Create a class challenge."
  • Simple class challenge link / code.
  • Basic teacher view: how many students joined / played.
  • CTA to request a School Hive, pilot pack or upgrade info.
  • Automated follow-up email to teachers after class activity.
  • Keep it lightweight — no full dashboard rebuild in the 6-week MVP. (Full spec in §09.)
Don't let the teacher bridge slow the player experience. For players, the primary CTAs stay challenge a friend, play again, share a win, climb / improve. Teacher prompts appear contextually and lightly.
Launch framing — teacher as player first. Don't lead with "give your students a challenge." First invite the teacher to play and discover the richness: "Play this for two minutes. You'll see why your students need it." Then the secondary CTA: "Run it with your class." The teacher should experience the game before being asked to administer it.
Still NOT in 6 weeks: ranked Hive Clash (unless it becomes a very clear unlock after initial signal) · full School super-admin automation · rebuilt teacher reporting · complex dashboards · deep integrations · cosmetics/seasons · full current-product redesign. We are patching the school pathway, not rebuilding the whole school product yet.
Ranked & retention nuance. Ranked may become the main retention engine — so if ranked is excluded from the first build, don't over-interpret weak D7 retention. First test challenge / rematch / play-again loops. If activation and challenge behaviour are strong, ranked becomes the obvious fast-follow. Keep ranked out of the first build, but don't judge D7 too harshly without it.

09 The Class Challenge bridge superseded, see note at top

This whole section describes the retired Class Challenge object (see the update note at the top of the doc). Kept below as reference for now since some of the thinking, particularly the privacy/data rules, may still be relevant once the direct-CTA flow is specced properly. Don't treat the levels, the v0 spec or the data model below as the current build target.

A Class Challenge (a.k.a. Hive Challenge) is a teacher-launched version of the B2C game. It is not a full class setup. It is a class-shaped challenge link that lets students play the same simple game experience under a teacher-created umbrella.

Three levels — build Level 1

Level 0
Public game link only. Fastest, but weak bridge, weak data.
Level 1 · MVP
Lightweight Class Challenge. Recommended.
Level 2
Full classroom mode. Later / paid B2B layer.
Build Level 1. Not a disconnected public game. Not a full classroom product.

The v0 spec

Teacher

  • Clicks "Run this with your class"
  • Enters name / email
  • Optionally adds school / class label
  • Receives challenge link / code

Student

  • Joins through the link / code
  • Enters initials / nickname or gets a generated handle
  • Plays the B2C game
  • No email. No public profile.

System

  • Tags activity with challenge_id
  • Records games played
  • Records basic engagement
  • Attaches activity to the teacher-created challenge

Teacher v0 view

  • Number of students joined
  • Number of games played
  • Repeat players (if easy)
  • CTA: "Want full class insight? Try Class Hive / School Hive / request pilot pack."

Privacy-shaped from day one

The bridge is a class-shaped share link — but it must be privacy-shaped from day one.
A public player link is for friends. A Class Challenge link is for students under a teacher umbrella. Same game engine, different data handling.

Student data rules for Class Challenge v0:

  • Students join under a teacher-created challenge.
  • Initials, nickname or safe generated handle only.
  • No student email · no full name · no date of birth.
  • No public student profile · no chat.
  • No public leaderboard with identifiable student info (unless designed safely).
  • Student activity scoped to the class challenge.
  • Teacher sees only basic activity in v0.
  • Richer student-level reporting stays inside the paid B2B product.

Data model flags

Teacher-supervised
challenge_type: teacher_class created_by_teacher: true student_data_mode: minimal public_visibility: off teacher_visible: aggregate_only challenge_id · teacher_id student_display_handle games_played · repeat_play · timestamps
Public friend
challenge_type: public_friend public share allowed no class umbrella no teacher view
This preserves the same game experience while respecting the different privacy expectations of public play vs teacher-supervised student play.

Tripwires

🟢Green. B2C loop is spreading and the school bridge is producing teacher/class signals → lean in (ranked, more loops, maybe a little spend).
🟡Yellow. Either B2C or the teacher bridge is working, but not both yet → don't overreact; tune the weak side.
🔴Red. Low activation, low retention, low sharing and low teacher intent → fix onboarding / magic / positioning before building more.
A weak 6-week viral result does not automatically invalidate the strategy. It may mean the email list was cold, the seed audience was wrong, the teacher framing was off, the share loop was weak, or the challenge wasn't clear enough. Diagnose before you pivot.
The maths to watch: K = i × c (invites per user × invite→signup rate). Amplification on any seed = 1 / (1 − K) — K=0.6 turns 1 player into 2.5, K=0.8 into 5. We don't need K>1; we need it as high as we can push it. Keep viral cycle time short — loops fire right after a match. Judge the slope and loop quality, not the headline number.

12 How much do we fix the current product?

Enough to stop warm demand leaking. Not enough to distract from testing the new growth hypothesis.

Rule: fix current-product friction only where it directly protects revenue or converts new demand.
The current B2B product remains the paid depth layer for now. We are not rebuilding it in the first six weeks. We are only patching the parts that would cause warm demand from the B2C / Class Challenge path to leak.

Worth doing now

  • Class setup / student join clarity
  • Clearer "what do I do first?"
  • Upgrade / payment pathway
  • School Hive positioning
  • Privacy / procurement page
  • Basic reporting clarity
  • Admin / billing fixes that save manual chaos

Not worth doing now

  • Full redesign
  • Perfect dashboards
  • Complex integrations
  • Full school-admin automation before signal
  • Advanced reporting rebuild
  • Every onboarding edge case
  • Cosmetic polish that doesn't lift activation/conversion
Don't build full Class Challenge reporting in the bridge if the current B2B product already contains the deeper reporting story. The bridge should create desire for the full product, not replace it.

15 The social campaign — the warm-up

The launch isn't a single "it's live" post — it's a 3-week, organic, story-led warm-up that earns belief before it asks for anything. Arc: Believe it → See it → Play it. Week 1 sells the idea (recall ≠ fluency); week 2 shows the game and the proof; week 3 builds the day-one crowd.

  • Why this shape: the DfE read says word-of-mouth and peer networks drive adoption when formal evidence is thin — so we lead with a belief worth sharing, not a hard sell. The free game is the word-of-mouth artifact.
  • One belief, three audiences: teachers (the engine + amplifiers — "stop drilling, start applying"), parents ("screen time you don't feel bad about"), and maths-curious adults ("chess, but built on numbers").
  • Channels (organic only): Instagram/Facebook · X/LinkedIn · email + community. Realistic founder cadence; repurpose one idea across surfaces.
  • Proof we already own: real testimonials from Pam Harris, Dan Finkel and Liesl McConchie.
