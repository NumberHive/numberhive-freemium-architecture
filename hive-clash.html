Internal concept · NumberHive

Hive Clash

Chess.com, but for mental maths. A ranked, head-to-head arena where adults discover they were always a maths person — by climbing a ladder, not sitting a test.

Concept summary · NumberHive · June 2026

In one line

Hive Clash is the competitive layer built on top of the Number Hive game. Where the prototype is a fun place to play, Hive Clash is a place to compete — real opponents, an ELO rating that travels with you, leaderboards, leagues and seasons. It turns a good game into an ongoing pursuit: maths as a skill you keep sharpening and a rank you earn, the same way chess.com turned chess into a daily habit for millions.

Schools framed maths as something you either had or didn't, and that belief follows most adults their whole lives. Hive Clash reframes it as a rank you climb and a score you can brag about — improvement you can feel, not a pass/fail verdict.

The game underneath

Both the prototype and Hive Clash share the same Number Hive mechanic — quick to learn, deep to master:

1

The board

59 cells — every product from 1×1 to 12×12. Both players share one board, so every cell is contested.

2

Your move

Two shared number pads, 1–12. Each turn you change one factor, shifting which cells your colour controls — attack or defend, but not both.

3

The win

First to claim four cells in a straight line wins. Simple rule, constant trade-offs — the strategy is in the tension.

What Hive Clash adds

⚔

Real opponents & matchmaking

You're matched against real players near your level. Ghost bots quietly fill the queue when no one's around, so a game is always instant.

★

A rating that means something

An ELO-style "Hive IQ" / global Hive rating that rises and falls with every game — the chess.com engine that makes each match matter.

⬢

Leagues, leaderboards & seasons

Climb visible tiers and reset each season for a fresh chase. Status and bragging rights are the reward.

↗

Play-a-friend & challenge links

Send a link by any means — a number on a whiteboard, a text, social — and you're playing someone you chose. No pairing with randoms unless you want it.

The chess.com parallel

What chess.com did for chess

  • Made a centuries-old game into a daily ranked habit
  • A rating you watch obsessively; opponents at your level
  • Practice that feels like competition, not study
  • Friends, leagues, seasons and endless one-more-game pull

What Hive Clash does for maths

  • Turns times-tables fluency into a ranked, repeatable pursuit
  • A Hive rating that proves you're getting better
  • Practice disguised as a game you actually want to win
  • The same retention loop, pointed at numeracy

The bet is the mechanic: it isn't about content or curriculum, it's about the fitness-and-strategy loop. People don't practise because they should — they practise because they want to climb.

Why it matters to the wider business

Building it is no longer the question — with AI, what used to be months of engineering is now days, so the work is design and product, not code. The real question is how big Hive Clash becomes. At minimum it's a powerful, viral top-of-funnel that pulls new players toward Number Hive (free game → network → teacher); at most, it's a significant consumer product in its own right. The competitive layer — rating, identity, leagues, challenge links — is what drives engagement, retention and student-led sharing that the casual prototype alone can't.

"I believe the Hive Clash concept with ELO scoring mechanism has viral potential. I think we need to sit down for a solid session and thrash it all around." — Chris, 17 June 2026

Open questions to settle

  • How direct is entry? Leaning toward "hit play and you're straight into a ranked game" — minimal friction before the first match.
  • Anonymous local-device scoring to start, vs. registration for a persistent rating — and when to nudge people to "carry this with you".
  • How hard to lean on competition vs. casual play for the free, top-of-funnel audience.
  • How big the consumer / B2C ambition should ultimately be — deliberately left open for now.
